What Do You Actually Need to Pass Matric in 2025? The Full Breakdown

📅 Updated 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read 🎓 Grade 12 · NSC

The NSC (National Senior Certificate) pass requirements are more nuanced than most students realise. "Passing Matric" means different things depending on what you want to do after school. Here's the complete, accurate breakdown.

The Six Performance Levels

The NSC uses a 7-level performance system:

  • Level 7 — Outstanding Achievement: 80–100%
  • Level 6 — Meritorious Achievement: 70–79%
  • Level 5 — Substantial Achievement: 60–69%
  • Level 4 — Adequate Achievement: 50–59%
  • Level 3 — Moderate Achievement: 40–49%
  • Level 2 — Elementary Achievement: 30–39%
  • Level 1 — Not Achieved: 0–29%

The Four Types of NSC Pass

1. NSC Pass (Basic)

Minimum requirements:

  • At least 40% in your Home Language
  • At least 30% in two other subjects
  • At least 40% in three other subjects (or all remaining subjects)

This gives you a basic NSC certificate but does not qualify you for university admission.

2. NSC Pass with Higher Certificate Admission

Minimum requirements:

  • At least 40% in your Home Language
  • At least 40% in three other designated subjects
  • At least 30% in two remaining subjects

Allows entry to certain Higher Certificate programmes at universities of technology and colleges.

3. NSC Pass with Diploma Admission

Minimum requirements:

  • At least 40% in your Home Language
  • At least 40% in four other subjects (including at least 3 designated subjects)
  • At least 30% in the remaining subjects

Opens entry to diploma programmes at universities of technology.

4. NSC Pass with Bachelor's Degree Admission (Most Competitive)

Minimum requirements:

  • At least 40% in Home Language
  • At least 50% in four recognised 20-credit subjects (including subjects on the Bachelor's admission list)
  • At least 30% in two remaining subjects

Important: A Bachelor's pass from the NSC only means you qualify to apply for university. Universities set their own much higher minimum Admission Point Score (APS) requirements — typically 28–42 APS for competitive degrees.

Understanding APS (Admission Point Score)

Most South African universities use APS to determine entry. Your APS is calculated from your 6 best subjects (excluding Life Orientation):

  • Level 7 (80–100%) = 7 points
  • Level 6 (70–79%) = 6 points
  • Level 5 (60–69%) = 5 points
  • Level 4 (50–59%) = 4 points
  • Level 3 (40–49%) = 3 points
  • Level 2 (30–39%) = 2 points
  • Level 1 (0–29%) = 1 point

Maximum APS: 42 points (6 subjects × 7 points each).

Typical APS Requirements at SA Universities

  • Medicine (MBChB) — UCT, UP, Wits: 40–42 APS (plus specific subject minimums)
  • Engineering — UP, Wits: 30–35 APS (plus Maths and Physical Sciences minimums)
  • Law — UCT, UKZN: 30–36 APS
  • Commerce/Business — UCT, Wits: 28–34 APS
  • Education — UNISA, UKZN: 24–28 APS

Subject Minimums That Matter

Many degrees require specific minimum percentages in certain subjects, regardless of your total APS:

  • Any engineering degree: Typically minimum 60–70% in Mathematics and Physical Sciences
  • Medicine: Minimum 70%+ in Mathematics and Physical Sciences at most universities
  • Accounting/Finance: Minimum 50–60% in Mathematics (not Maths Literacy)
  • Teaching: Minimum 50% in the subject(s) you want to teach

What About SBA (School-Based Assessment)?

Your final NSC mark is a combination of your School-Based Assessment (SBA) and your external examination marks:

  • Most subjects: 25% SBA + 75% external exam
  • Life Orientation: 100% SBA (no external exam)
  • Practical subjects (CAT, IT, Consumer Studies, etc.): SBA weighting is higher (typically 40–75%)

Key implication: Your SBA marks are set before the exam. If your SBA average is already 70% and you do well in the exam, your final mark cannot fall below a certain level. Protect your SBA marks throughout the year — they are not recoverable.

The Supplementary Exam

If you fail one or two subjects, you may write supplementary exams (usually held in February/March of the following year). These give you a second chance to improve your NSC qualification without repeating the full year. The supplementary exam covers the same CAPS content as the original November exams.
