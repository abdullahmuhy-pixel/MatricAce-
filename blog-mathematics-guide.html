Grade 10–12 Mathematics: The Topics You Cannot Afford to Skip

📅 Updated 2025 ⏱️ 8 min read 🎓 Grade 10–12 · CAPS

NSC Mathematics is written across two papers: Paper 1 (150 marks, 3 hours) and Paper 2 (150 marks, 3 hours). Together they test 12 main topic areas, but they are not equally weighted. Knowing which topics carry the most marks — and focusing your energy there — is the most strategic thing you can do in the weeks before exams.

Paper 1 High-Value Topics

Algebra, Equations & Inequalities (~25 marks)

This is the foundation of everything. Quadratic equations, simultaneous equations, nature of roots using the discriminant (Δ = b²−4ac), and quadratic inequalities all appear here. If you can't factorise fluently and work with the discriminant confidently, the rest of the paper suffers. This topic appears in Grade 10, 11, and 12 with increasing depth.

Grade 12 tip: The discriminant question (find values of k for equal/no real roots) appears in virtually every NSC Paper 1. It's worth 4–6 marks and is completely predictable. Master it.

Functions & Graphs (~35 marks)

The single highest-weighted topic in Paper 1. You must be fluent with six function families: linear, quadratic (parabola), hyperbola, exponential, logarithm, and square root. For each you need to: sketch the graph, find all key features (intercepts, asymptotes, turning points, domain, range), describe transformations, and find the equation from given information.

Inverse functions — swapping x and y and understanding what happens graphically — are a Grade 11 and 12 speciality that examiners love.

Finance, Growth & Decay (~15 marks)

Compound interest (A = P(1+i)ⁿ), depreciation, effective vs nominal interest rates, and annuities (future value and present value). These questions are formulaic — if you know which formula to apply and how to substitute correctly, they are free marks. Many students neglect this topic and lose 15 easy marks.

Calculus (~35 marks)

Differentiation using the power rule, finding stationary points, second derivative test (f''(x) determines maximum or minimum), sketching cubic functions using derivatives, and optimisation word problems. Calculus questions are heavily structured — each step is marked independently, so partial marks are available even if you don't reach the final answer.

Sequences & Series (~25 marks)

Arithmetic sequences (constant difference, general term Tₙ = a+(n−1)d, sum Sₙ), geometric sequences (constant ratio, Tₙ = arⁿ⁻¹, sum formula), and sum to infinity (S∞ = a/(1−r) when |r|<1). These are highly predictable and well-worth mastering completely.

Paper 2 High-Value Topics

Statistics (~20 marks)

Measures of central tendency and spread (mean, median, standard deviation), box-and-whisker plots, histograms, and bivariate data analysis including scatter plots, regression lines, and correlation. Many students underestimate this section — it's 20 marks of relatively accessible content.

Trigonometry (~40 marks)

The biggest topic in Paper 2. This includes: general solutions (solving trig equations for all solutions), compound angle formulas (must be memorised — not on the formula sheet), reduction formulas, trig graphs with transformations, and 2D/3D problems using the sine rule, cosine rule, and area rule.

Critical note: The compound angle and double angle formulas are NOT given on the formula sheet. They must be committed to memory: sin(A+B) = sinAcosB + cosAsinB, cos(A+B) = cosAcosB − sinAsinB, etc.

Analytical Geometry (~40 marks)

Circle equations (x−a)² + (y−b)² = r², tangent lines to circles (tangent ⊥ radius), midpoints, distance formula, gradient formula, and proving geometric properties of quadrilaterals using coordinates. Examiners love combining circle geometry with tangent/normal questions for 15–20 marks.

Euclidean Geometry (~40 marks, includes proofs)

Circle theorems, similarity and proportionality proofs, and the Theorem of Pythagoras proof. Many students avoid geometry because of proofs, but the 6-mark proof questions follow predictable patterns. Learn the 5 or 6 most commonly tested proofs.

Sample Questions — Test Yourself

Try these typical NSC-style questions:

Grade 12 · Paper 1 · Functions (2 marks)
The discriminant of the equation x² + kx + 4 = 0 is equal to zero. The value of k is:
✅ Δ = b²−4ac = k²−4(1)(4) = k²−16 = 0 → k² = 16 → k = ±4. Equal roots when Δ = 0.
Grade 12 · Paper 1 · Calculus (3 marks)
The gradient of the tangent to f(x) = 2x³ − 3x at x = 1 is:
✅ f'(x) = 6x² − 3. At x=1: f'(1) = 6(1)² − 3 = 6 − 3 = 3. Differentiate using the power rule first.

Study Strategy by Grade

Grade 10

Prioritise: Algebraic expressions and factorisation, introduction to functions (especially linear and quadratic), trigonometry basics (SOHCAHTOA and special angles), and analytical geometry (distance, midpoint, gradient). These are the building blocks for everything in Grades 11 and 12.

Grade 11

Prioritise: Quadratic inequalities and the discriminant, sequences and series (arithmetic and geometric), functions and transformations (including inverses), and trigonometry (CAST diagram, reduction formulas, sine and cosine rules).

Grade 12

Prioritise: Calculus (35 marks in Paper 1 alone), functions including logs and exponentials (35 marks), trigonometry for Paper 2 (40 marks), and analytical geometry (40 marks). Together these four topics account for over 160 of 300 available marks.
